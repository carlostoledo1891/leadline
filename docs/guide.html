the guide

What does each number rest on?

Every number on a metocean figure rests on something: the record, a fit, a choice, or nothing at all. Leadline asks the figure to say which. This guide classifies the numbers a pipeline usually draws, each with an example from the gallery.

Four questions to ask of any number

  1. Could I get it back exactly from the record? A value as recorded, a count, a maximum, an order statistic. It is decided: solid.
  2. Did a fit, a solver or someone's printout produce it? It is computed: dashed.
  3. Did a person, a default or a standard pick it? A threshold, a bin, a return period, a plotting position. It is chosen: dotted, and the figure names the pick in italic, given p = 0.99.
  4. Is there nothing to show? Missing hours, a refused fit. It is refused: no mark, a hatched void, ∅ and words.

A mark is as strong as the weakest number on its path. A QQ point pairs an exact monthly maximum with a fitted quantile, so the curve through those points is computed. A storm peak is the exact maximum of its run, but the runs come from a chosen threshold and separation, so the peak is decided given those choices, and the figure says so.

And beneath every number: the premises

A decided count still rests on the record being right; a computed contour also rests on its model, its state duration and its method. Leadline's premise ladder (lm.premises) lists them in the order the number travelled through them and names the weakest: see the return-level curve and the contour in the gallery.
